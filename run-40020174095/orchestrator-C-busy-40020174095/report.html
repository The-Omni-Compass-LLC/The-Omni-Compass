Omni-Compass's own orchestrator: the first showing

Built from scratch on 23 October 2026: Python's standard library and Omni-Compass's own compass law, no code of Kubernetes or any other orchestrator. Run on this one machine, every piece a real process, every answer checked. Local only, not pushed. Run and scored by The Omni-Compass LLC's own code. No outside party has reviewed or approved these results.

The rig. Six machines of half a processor each, walled by the kernel's own CPU quotas (two machines on each of three cores); the control plane, the front, the six machines' hands and the clients on the fourth core. The service's units: 250 millicores each (request and limit). Each request: a chain of 97,218 SHA-256 hashes (about 40 ms of processor), its digest checked by the client. The clients: closed loop, 0.1 s of thought between requests, stepping by one.

1. The yardstick against Omni, the same clients

Yardstick: Kubernetes' published autoscaling rule (every 15 s; 50% of the request; 10% tolerance; a fall only to the highest count of the last 300 s), the units spread as Kubernetes' scheduler spreads them, all six machines on, as a fixed cluster runs. Written by us from its documentation as the measure; it is not Kubernetes itself. Omni: the compass law on the 95th percentile every 5 s, never below its native twin (the yardstick's rule on the same readings), units packed, machines parked and woken (two at least; a parked machine takes 10 s to come back).
